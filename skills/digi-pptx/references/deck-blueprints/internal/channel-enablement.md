# Channel partner training blueprint

**Reader:** a partner's sales rep selling Digi through their own motion.
**Their job after the deck:** recognize a deal, pitch it from memory, beat the
competitor they will actually meet, and know what to ask. Abstracted from the
Remote Reach channel training deck.

**The spine of a channel deck is portability.** The rep does not work for Digi
and will not reread the deck. Everything has to be memorable and self-contained:
anchors they can recite, a named competitor list, discovery questions, objection
answers. If a slide cannot be remembered, it will not be used.

## Archetype sequence

| # | Slide | Purpose | Layout / visual | Voice |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Title** | Product, "Channel Partner Training," org, GA date | template title | — |
| 2 | **The phone call you can't ignore** | Open on the visceral pain scenario, not the product | **illustration** (the bad-day scene) + short text | tense, concrete, story |
| 3 | **What you do Monday** | The immediate action: who to call, what to open, when to pull in a Digi SE + resource links | text + resource links | imperative, "do this" |
| 4 | **X in one minute** | The elevator: what it is, the session/feature list, what it is NOT (no agent, no extra SKU) | bullets + **illustration** | brisk |
| 5 | **Who buys it. Who uses it.** | The 3 segments with concrete device examples + the personas in the room | 3-column or bullets + **illustration** | segment-named |
| 6 | **Why it works on a bad day** | The 2-3 architectural choices that matter, in order, as differentiators | numbered bullets + **illustration** (architecture) | "in order:" |
| 7 | **Two anchors to memorize** | The 2 things a rep must retain to pitch at all | two bold anchors, text | "if you remember these two things…" |
| 8 | **The competitors to know** | Name the 2-3 real competitors and their one shared weakness | per-competitor rows + **illustration** | named, factual |
| 9 | **Where X wins** | The capability comparison table: Digi vs Others | comparison table (text) | check/x, terse |
| 10 | **How it's packaged** | The commercial story: included vs add-on, one line item, who to see before quoting | text | "no upsell motion required" |
| 11 | **What to ask your prospect** | The 5 discovery questions that surface the pain | numbered questions, text | verbatim questions |
| 12 | **The top objections** | The 3-4 partner-side objections with the reframe | objection + reframe pairs, text | "let's talk about your traffic flow" |
| — | **Thank You** | contact | template closing | — |

## What to swap per product

The pain scenario (slide 2), the elevator (slide 4), the segments + device
examples (slide 5), the architectural anchors (slides 6-7), the named competitors
and the comparison rows (slides 8-9), packaging (slide 10), the discovery
questions (slide 11), and the objections (slide 12). The *moves* are constant.

## Guardrails

- **Anchors are the product.** Slide 7 is the one slide that has to survive in a
  rep's head a week later. Make it two things, no more.
- **Name competitors plainly** but stick to verifiable capability gaps (no FUD).
  An unverified "we're first" or "we're the only one who can" claim is exactly
  the kind of line that comes back to bite a rep in front of a customer who
  already knows better — verify before it goes on the slide.
- **No prices in writing.** Packaging is "included vs add-on, see your Digi rep
  before quoting numbers." Assume the deck travels to the partner's customer.
- **Open on pain, not product.** The channel deck that opens with a feature list
  is forgotten; the one that opens with the dark-site phone call is repeated.
