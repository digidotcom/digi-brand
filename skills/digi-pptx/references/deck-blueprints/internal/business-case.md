# Business case / funding request blueprint

**Reader:** whoever controls the budget or the headcount, plus the finance
partner who will pressure-test the numbers. **Their job after the deck:**
fund it, decline it, or tell you what would make it fundable.

**The spine of a business case is competition, not merit.** The ask is not
competing against "is this good" — it is competing against every other use
of the same money, including doing nothing at all. The case has to show this
beats the alternatives, doing-nothing included, and show the arithmetic that
proves it, not just assert it.

## Archetype sequence

| # | Slide | Purpose | Layout / visual | Voice |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Title** | The ask in one line, presenter, org, date | template title | — |
| 2 | **The cost of doing nothing** | What the status quo already costs, quantified, before any Digi ask appears | stat callouts + **illustration** | numbers, not adjectives |
| 3 | **What we're proposing** | The one-breath statement of the request | short line, text | plain, no jargon |
| 4 | **Alternatives considered** | Buy vs. build vs. partner vs. status quo, and why each loses except the recommendation | comparison-table layout (33/34): option / why it loses | factual, even-handed |
| 5 | **The model** | Cost, timeline, and expected return — placeholders, no real figures in this blueprint | comparison-table layout (33/34) or stat callouts | sourced, comparable |
| 6 | **What this assumes** | The 2-3 assumptions the answer is most sensitive to, named explicitly | numbered list, text | candid, "the case rests on…" |
| 7 | **Payback and the downside case** | When it pays back if right, and what happens to the ask if the key assumption is wrong | two scenarios, text or stat callouts | direct, both cases shown |
| 8 | **What we're asking for** | A specific, sized commitment — amount, headcount, or duration, never "support" | single clear ask, text | "we are asking for X, for Y duration" |
| 9 | **What happens next** | The decision needed, by when, and what unblocks once it's made | text | imperative |
| — | **Thank You** | contact | template closing | — |

## What to swap per request

The status-quo cost (slide 2), the proposal (slide 3), the alternatives
evaluated (slide 4), the model inputs (slide 5), the sensitive assumptions
(slide 6), the payback and downside scenario (slide 7), and the sized ask
(slide 8). The competing-against-alternatives spine and the doing-nothing
baseline are constant regardless of what is being funded.

## What to cut when time is short

1. **Cut slide 4 (alternatives considered) to one line per option** first —
   fold it under slide 3 as a "why not X, Y, Z" bullet rather than losing it;
   presenting only the chosen option looks like the alternatives were never
   evaluated.
2. **Merge slides 6 and 7** into a single "what this assumes, and what
   happens if we're wrong" slide for a short-form pitch.
3. **Never cut slide 2 (cost of doing nothing).** Without it every number
   later in the deck reads as pure spend with nothing to weigh it against.
4. **Never cut slide 7's downside case.** A case with no downside scenario
   shown is a pitch, not a business case, and a sharp finance partner will
   ask for it live if it isn't already there.

## Layout notes

Same template mechanics as the other internal blueprints
(`2024-Digi-Confidential-PPT-Template.potx`, zones from [../../visuals.md](../../visuals.md)).
Slide 5's model and slide 7's scenarios favor stat-callout pairs
(status quo vs. proposed, base case vs. downside) over a dense table — the
reviewer should be able to read the comparison at a glance, with the full
model available on request rather than crowded onto the slide.

## Voice

Plain, arithmetic, no salesmanship. State the comparison and let the numbers
argue the case rather than adjectives. Same brand voice rules as every
blueprint (plain, professional, no dashes) — see
[../README.md](../README.md#voice).

## Who reviews this before the room does

A business case that reaches its budget owner without a finance partner's
eyes on it first invites the arithmetic to be re-litigated live instead of
approved. Note, in slide 5 or as a footer, who has already validated the
model — this is the same move as sourcing a number on a leadership-review
slide: it moves the conversation from "is this number right" to "given this
number, is this worth funding."

## Guardrails — this repo is public

- **No real prices, margins, or internal cost figures anywhere in the
  blueprint or its examples.** This teaches the structure; the user supplies
  their own numbers when they build the real deck. Use `{{PLACEHOLDER}}`
  shapes for any figure in an example slide.
- **State the cost of doing nothing before any other number appears.**
  Skipping slide 2 is the single most common reason a business case reads as
  "we want to spend money" instead of "the current path is already costing
  us."
- **Show the alternatives, including buy-versus-build and
  partner-versus-build, and name why each loses.** Presenting only the
  chosen path makes it look like nothing else was considered, which invites
  exactly that question in the room.
- **Name the assumptions the model is most sensitive to.** Every model rests
  on assumptions; hiding them means the reviewer finds them anyway and stops
  trusting the rest of the deck. Two or three, named plainly, beats a dense
  appendix nobody reads.
- **Ask for a specific, sized commitment**, not "support" or "buy-in." An
  amount, a headcount, or a duration — something the room can actually
  approve or decline.
