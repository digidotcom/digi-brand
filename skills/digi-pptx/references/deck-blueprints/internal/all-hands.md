# All-hands / town hall blueprint

**Reader:** the whole company or a whole business unit — every function, every
level, wildly mixed context in one room. **Their job after the deck:** know
what changed, what it means for them, and what leadership is actually worried
about.

**The spine of an all-hands is trust, not status.** The room is not there to
learn the quarter's numbers; it is scanning for whether leadership is being
straight with them. A deck of green checkmarks reads as spin and spends
credibility instead of building it. Every slide should survive the question
"would this hold up if someone in the room already knows the bad part?"

## Archetype sequence

| # | Slide | Purpose | Layout / visual | Voice |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Title** | "[Company/BU] All-Hands," quarter or date, presenter | template title | — |
| 2 | **The thing everyone's already talking about** | Address the layoff, the miss, the reorg, or the rumor head-on, before anything else | short direct statement, text | plain, no euphemism |
| 3 | **Where we stand** | The 2-3 numbers that matter this period, each with a one-line so-what | stat callouts + **illustration** (optional) | numbers, not adjectives |
| 4 | **What went right** | Named wins, tied to named work | bullets, text | specific, credit-giving |
| 5 | **What didn't** | The misses, stated as plainly as the wins — no hedging language | bullets, text | candid, "here's what we got wrong" |
| 6 | **What this means for you** | Translate the company-level picture into the day-to-day for someone not in leadership, by function | segment bullets, text | "if you're in [function], this means…" |
| 7 | **What's next** | The 2-3 priorities for the coming period, in plain language | numbered bullets + **illustration** | forward-looking, concrete |
| 8 | **Recognition** | Named people, named work — never a generic "great job everyone" | names + what they did, text | warm, specific |
| 9 | **Ask us anything** | Open the floor; note how questions submitted in advance were handled | prompt slide, text | "take the hard ones first" |
| — | **Thank You** | contact / where to send follow-up questions | template closing | — |

## What to swap per session

The opening address (slide 2, or cut it if there is genuinely nothing the
room is already talking about), the period's numbers (slide 3), the wins and
misses (slides 4-5), the by-function translation (slide 6), the priorities
(slide 7), and the recognition list (slide 8). The trust-first spine and the
open-Q&A close are constant.

## What to cut when time is short

1. **Cut slide 6 (what this means for you) to one bullet per function**
   first — fold it into slide 7 as a closing line per priority rather than
   losing it outright; it is the slide that keeps the deck from feeling
   like it was written for leadership about leadership.
2. **Cut slide 8 (recognition)** to a single slide of names with no
   elaboration if time is very short — never cut it to zero. A missed
   all-hands recognition is felt.
3. **Never cut slide 2 (the thing everyone's talking about) or slide 5
   (what didn't).** These are the two slides that make the rest of the deck
   credible; cutting either turns the remaining wins into spin by default.
4. **Never shrink the Q&A window to add slides.** A rushed Q&A reads as
   avoidance regardless of what was on the slides before it.

## Layout notes

Same template mechanics as the other internal blueprints
(`digi-template.pptx`, zones from [../../visuals.md](../../visuals.md)):
title + short left column + one visual per slide where a visual earns its
place, callout band for the takeaway. Slides 2 and 5 are usually text-only —
the content is the plain statement itself, not a supporting graphic. Slide 3
favors a stat-callout row (see `visuals.md`) over a chart if the exact
figures are not yet public.

## Voice

Direct, first-person plural ("we"), no corporate distancing language.
Say what happened, not what "occurred." A sentence that needs "unfortunately"
or "challenging environment" to soften bad news should instead just say the
news. Same brand voice rules as every blueprint (plain, professional, no
dashes) — see [../README.md](../README.md#voice).

## Where questions go after the room empties

Not everyone in the audience will ask their question live, and some
questions arrive after the deck is already out. Name, on the closing slide,
where a question submitted afterward actually goes and roughly when it gets
answered — a live-only Q&A quietly excludes anyone remote, anyone who froze
up in the room, and anyone who thought of the question on the walk back to
their desk.

## Guardrails

- **Slide 2 exists whenever there is a live rumor, miss, or reorg — never
  optional in that case.** Burying the known issue on slide 14 means nobody
  hears slides 3 through 20; the room has already checked out by then.
- **No unreleased financial figures on any slide.** Digi is publicly traded;
  anything that touches unreleased revenue, margin, or guidance routes
  through whoever owns disclosure before it goes on a slide, not through
  this deck. When in doubt, state the qualitative direction and route the
  number.
- **Recognition is specific or it is cut.** "Great job everyone" is worth
  less than saying nothing — it signals nobody was actually tracked. If
  there is no specific credit to give this cycle, skip the slide rather
  than fill it with a generic line.
- **A Q&A slide that only shows softball questions is a red flag, not a
  deliverable.** If prepared questions are shown, include at least one hard
  one and how it was answered.
