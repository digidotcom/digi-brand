# Project kickoff blueprint

**Reader:** the cross-functional group about to do the work — engineering,
marketing, sales, support, whoever is in scope. **Their job after the
deck:** know what they own, what "done" means, and who decides when it is
contested.

**The spine of a kickoff is preventing month-three arguments.** Scope
boundaries, decision rights, and the definition of done are cheap to settle
in one room now and expensive to relitigate later once work is already
underway. Every slide should be answering a question that would otherwise
surface as a conflict weeks in.

## Archetype sequence

| # | Slide | Purpose | Layout / visual | Voice |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Title** | Project name, "Kickoff," org, date | template title | — |
| 2 | **Why this, why now** | The forcing function and the outcome this project exists to produce | short statement, text | plain, "here's why we're doing this" |
| 3 | **What's in scope** | The concrete deliverables this project covers | bullets, text | specific, checkable |
| 4 | **What's explicitly out of scope** | Named exclusions — the things this project will NOT do, stated as plainly as slide 3 | bullets, text | equally specific, "not this" |
| 5 | **What done looks like** | The definition of done, agreed before the schedule is set | criteria list, text | testable, unambiguous |
| 6 | **Workstreams and owners** | One owner per workstream, named — never a team | comparison-table layout (33/34): workstream / owner / deliverable | one name per row |
| 7 | **Timeline** | Milestones tied to the definition-of-done criteria from slide 5 | timeline graphic or table + **illustration** | dated, concrete |
| 8 | **Dependencies outside this room** | The people and teams this project needs but who are not in the kickoff | dependency + owner + what's needed, text | explicit, "we need X from Y by Z" |
| 9 | **Decision rights** | Who decides on a contested call, and the escalation path if the decider is unavailable | text | crisp, declarative |
| 10 | **Risks** | Named risks, each with an owner and the trigger that means it's happening | risk + owner + trigger, text | candid |
| 11 | **How we'll communicate** | Cadence, channel, and where status lives between now and the next checkpoint | text | directory |
| — | **Thank You** | contact | template closing | — |

## What to swap per project

The forcing function (slide 2), the in-scope and out-of-scope lists (slides
3-4), the definition of done (slide 5), the workstreams and owners (slide
6), the timeline (slide 7), the external dependencies (slide 8), the
decision-maker (slide 9), and the risk list (slide 10). The
scope/done/owner/decision-rights spine is constant across every project this
blueprint serves.

## What to cut when time is short

1. **Cut slide 11 (how we'll communicate) first** — state the cadence as a
   single line under slide 9 instead of giving it a dedicated slide.
2. **Merge slides 9 and 10** into one "risks and decision rights" slide for
   a 20-minute kickoff.
3. **Never cut slide 4 (explicitly out of scope).** Unstated exclusions are
   the single largest source of late-project conflict; people fill silence
   with their own assumptions, and by the time it surfaces the work is
   already underway on the wrong assumption.
4. **Never cut slide 6 (workstreams and owners) or compress it to team
   names.** A workstream listed against a team rather than a person is
   unowned in practice, even if it looks staffed on the slide.

## Layout notes

Same template mechanics as the other internal blueprints
(`2024-Digi-Confidential-PPT-Template.potx`, zones from [../../visuals.md](../../visuals.md)).
Slide 6 (workstreams and owners) and slide 10 (risks) favor a `full-band`
table over bullets — both are structurally a table (row per workstream, row
per risk) and shrinking them to fit a half-width column is how the "one
owner per row" discipline quietly slips.

## Voice

Plain and declarative — this deck exists to remove ambiguity, so hedged
language defeats its purpose. Say "X owns Y" and "done means Z," not "X will
generally be responsible for" or "done should look roughly like." Same brand
voice rules as every blueprint (plain, professional, no dashes) — see
[../README.md](../README.md#voice).

## Guardrails

- **Say what is out of scope, explicitly and by name, on its own slide.**
  Folding it into the in-scope slide as a footnote defeats the purpose;
  give it the same visual weight as what's included.
- **One owner per workstream, named — never a team or "TBD."** If a
  workstream genuinely has no owner yet, say so plainly on the slide rather
  than leaving it implied; a visible gap gets fixed, a hidden one gets
  discovered in month three.
- **Definition of done comes before the timeline, every time.** A date with
  no agreed finish line attached to it is a wish, not a plan — slide 5 has
  to land before slide 7 makes sense.
- **Name the decision-maker for contested calls, and the escalation path if
  they're unavailable.** Every project hits a tie at some point; deciding
  the tiebreak now costs one slide and saves a stalled week later.
- **Risks need an owner and a trigger, not a generic list.** "Vendor delay"
  with no owner and no named trigger is a note to self, not a managed risk;
  name who watches for it and what event means it's live.
- **Surface dependencies on people who are not in the room, by name.** This
  is the most common way a kickoff schedule turns out to be fiction — the
  plan assumed availability from someone who was never asked.
