# Direct sales enablement blueprint

**Reader:** a Digi direct seller (quota carrier). **Their job after the deck:**
spot the opportunity in a normal conversation, say what it is in one breath, and
defend it against the objections they will always hear. Abstracted from the
Remote Reach sales enablement deck.

**The spine of a sales deck is the three moves: spot it, say it, defend it.** A
seller does not need architecture; they need to recognize the opening, deliver a
clean line, and not fumble the pushback. Everything serves "in front of a
customer, can they do the move."

## Archetype sequence

| # | Slide | Purpose | Layout / visual | Voice |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## What to swap per product

The one-breath line (slide 3), the status-quo pains (slide 4), the segments + ASK
questions (slide 6), the three wins (slide 7), the buy paths (slide 8), the talk
track pains (slide 9), the objections (slide 10), the demo beats (slide 11), and
the say/not-that pairs (slide 12). The three-moves frame and the talk-track shape
are constant.

## Guardrails

- **"Say this, not that" (slide 12) is mandatory** and is where compliance lives:
  no prices, no codenames, no roadmap promises in writing. Assume the seller's
  words travel. This slide is the single best leverage point for keeping a launch
  on-message.
- **Objections reframe to "it tightens posture," never "trust us."** Security is
  the first objection every time; the answer is contained-path + full logging +
  no standing tunnel, not reassurance.
- **Lead with the eliminated truck roll, not the technology** (slide 9). The whole
  deck fails if the seller pitches architecture instead of outcome.
- **Demo is the close** (slide 11): "seeing serial-in-a-browser is what closes the
  loop." Always provide a backup recording placeholder in case live env is down.
